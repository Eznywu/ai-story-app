Old lamp keeper Maren opens the lighthouse only when the fog sings.

For fifty years she climbed the spiral alone, trimming wicks while the town below
pretended the beam was automatic. The night her hands shook too much to strike a match,
a girl appeared on the gallery with a jar of fireflies — not foolish magic, but stubborn
light. The storm had stolen the main lens; together they wired the jar into the old
Fresnel cradle, and the beam returned thin but true.

The council came at dawn, ready to retire Maren. Instead they found her teaching the child
how to read the horizon — proof that some responsibilities are not replaced, only handed
forward like a warm lens held in steady hands.
